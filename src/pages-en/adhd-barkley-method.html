When everyday life with your child becomes a battle

Instructions repeated ten times. Homework that ends in tears. Meltdowns when it’s time to leave, go to bed or switch off the screen. And the feeling that you’ve already tried everything.

If this sounds familiar, you are not a bad parent, and you are not alone. A child with ADHD, or a child who pushes back a lot, needs a different kind of support. And that different approach can be learned.

That is exactly what the Barkley® programme offers: practical, proven tools you can start using in the very first week.

The Barkley® programme

A recognised parent training programme

Dr Russell A. Barkley, an American researcher and one of the most cited names in ADHD research, developed this programme for parents of children with ADHD, oppositional defiant disorder (ODD), or both. It belongs to the family of parent training programmes, based on cognitive behavioural principles.

The ADHD guidelines used in Switzerland recommend a multimodal approach, in which supporting parents plays a full part.

What the programme gives you

  • Understand what ADHD is and why your child reacts the way they do
  • Give instructions your child hears – and follows more often
  • Praise good behaviour so that it happens again
  • Set clear boundaries, with predictable consequences and without shouting
  • Ease the key moments: mornings, homework, bedtime, outings
  • Defuse meltdowns and reduce conflict
  • Enjoy being together again

Nobody is asking you to become a perfect parent. The goal: solid landmarks and tools that fit your real family life.

How it works

How the coaching works

A discovery call (20 min, free)

You tell me about your situation, I answer your questions, and together we see whether the programme can help your family.

Ten one-hour sessions

For parents only, at the practice or by video call, at a pace of about two sessions a month.

Practice at home

Between sessions, you put one specific tool into practice. This practice is what makes the difference. Six months after the end, a free follow-up session lets us take stock.

The ten sessions

  1. Understanding ADHD and defiance
  2. Observing what triggers and maintains difficult behaviour
  3. Special time: reconnecting with your child
  4. Clear, effective instructions
  5. Points or token systems
  6. Managing time and transitions
  7. Time-out: stopping a behaviour without escalation
  8. Homework and working with the school
  9. Staying on course outside: shops, visits, outings
  10. Review and planning ahead

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about ADHD and the programme

What is ADHD?

It is a neurodevelopmental condition: attention, activity levels and impulse control work differently, to the point of making school, relationships and family life harder. It shows up in childhood and often persists into the teenage years and adulthood.

What are the signs of ADHD in children?

Every child has their own profile. Common signs include: difficulty staying focused or finishing what they start, forgetfulness and lost belongings, restlessness, impatience and impulsive reactions. It’s not the number of signs that matters, but their intensity, how long they last and their impact on daily life.

Is a child with ADHD always hyperactive?

No. Some children mainly have attention difficulties, others mainly restlessness and impulsivity, others both. In girls, ADHD more often goes unnoticed: inattention is less visible and restlessness is sometimes more internal. Diagnosis therefore often comes later.

What is oppositional defiant disorder (ODD)?

It is a frequent and lasting tendency (more than six months) towards anger, arguing about rules and confrontation with adults, to the point of weighing on the whole family’s daily life. All children push back at times; with ODD, it is persistent. ODD can go hand in hand with ADHD, but not necessarily.

Is ADHD caused by poor parenting?

No. ADHD has mainly genetic and neurobiological origins. However, the way a child is supported has a big influence on how their difficulties show up day to day. That is exactly where the programme works.

Is a diagnosis needed to follow the programme?

No. The programme is also for parents of a child who is restless, impulsive, inattentive or very defiant, even without a diagnosis.

Does the programme replace medical or psychological care?

No. It is an educational programme for parents. It does not diagnose and is not psychotherapy. Follow-up by your paediatrician, a child psychiatrist or a psychologist remains essential; the programme complements it.

Can I follow the programme by video call?

Yes. Video sessions have the same content as at the practice. It is often the easiest option for couples with busy schedules.

My child is a teenager – is the programme suitable?

This programme is designed for children. For a teenager, get in touch: we will look together at what is appropriate.

Are sessions covered by basic or supplementary health insurance?

This programme is not covered by compulsory health insurance (LAMal) or by supplementary complementary-medicine insurance.
